# Product measurement

Before interpreting results, define the decision and original hypothesis.
For each selected metric, record its calculation, numerator, denominator, population, exclusions, source, observation period, and owner.
Record the baseline, desired change, guardrail metrics, and the evidence supporting any threshold.
Guardrail metrics detect unacceptable effects outside the primary outcome.

Check event definitions, missing data, duplicate events, delayed processing, and changes in measurement.
Compare equivalent populations and periods.
Explain changes in acquisition, eligibility, seasonality, or exposure that limit comparison.
Keep qualitative evidence linked to its actual sample.

An observed association does not establish that a release caused a metric change.
State whether evidence comes from a controlled experiment or an observational comparison.
For experiments, record assignment, exposure, sample assumptions, stopping policy, and uncertainty.
Use a checked statistical implementation for calculations.
Do not extend an experiment solely to obtain statistical significance.
Report insufficient evidence when the design, sample, or data cannot support the requested conclusion.

Recommend continue, revise, stop, or gather evidence with a concrete reason.
Separate release-health action from longer-term product decisions.
Do not deploy, publish, notify others, or change production measurement configuration without applicable authorization.
