# Discovery decisions

When comparing opportunities, identify the affected audience, demonstrated consequence, supporting evidence, strategic constraints, dependencies, and cost of delay.
Compare the current experience as an explicit option.
Explain priority qualitatively when effort or impact cannot be measured.
Record selected, deferred, and rejected opportunities with their reasons.

For interviews, identify the decision and uncertainty before writing questions.
Ask about actual past behavior, context, workarounds, and consequences.
Keep prompts neutral and avoid treating stated preference as demonstrated demand.
Record counterexamples and source IDs for later synthesis.

For experiments, identify the assumption most likely to change the decision.
Choose a bounded method that can supply the missing evidence.
State the audience, hypothesis, measurement, success and failure conditions, limits, and stopping policy.
Challenge value, usability, feasibility, and viability assumptions with available contrary evidence.
Do not invent likelihood estimates or claim that a prototype demonstrates production readiness.

The project owns research access, experiment approval, product priorities, and publication.
Create external tickets only when the requested next action needs them.
